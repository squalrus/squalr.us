## Claude and the turn

[The day you picked it up for a personal project and it clicked — not as something driving the work, but as an extension of yourself. The shift from "AI decides" to "I leverage it" — force multiplier for your own projects, on your own terms, organized the way you wanted.]

Specific points to land in this section:

- It finally enabled the kind of upfront planning I'd always wanted to do but rarely had time for — real design docs, not just diving in.
- Pulling in code samples and references during planning feels like the Stack Overflow copy-paste era, just moved earlier in the process — into the design phase instead of the "I'm stuck, let me Google it" phase.
- Working with it is like having both a junior and a senior developer on call. Hand it the small repetitive stuff a junior would take, but it can also tackle complex problems or explain code in the kind of depth a senior would.
- None of that works without keeping real software engineering discipline in place: small features, PRs, code review, asking it to explain itself, writing documentation as you go. That discipline is what keeps a project usable with or without AI — a human can pick it up and take over at any point.
- The mental model that makes it click: treat it as an extension of yourself and how you already organize a project, not a "do everything for me" black box.
- The caveat that matters most: this is a force multiplier when you already have a strong foundational understanding of the domain — web, games, whatever — and AI is doing the legwork underneath that. It's genuinely concerning for newcomers to the industry, because AI will produce *a* website for anyone who asks, but it can't substitute for the judgment to know when the output is wrong. Ask it for something and it'll always hand you something back — the real test is what happens when it hits a bug it can't solve and starts layering fix after fix without actually fixing anything. That's just tech debt with extra steps. Catching that requires the expertise AI can't give you.
